# Build the outreach agent incumbents won't

The prototype should look like Zamp's own "GTM Associate" AI employee doing a finance-ops buyer's research. A Zamp-style outreach build should take a prospect, gather a handful of public, dated, cited signals from legal sources (Tavily or Serper for news, Jina Reader for company pages, keyless Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby job APIs, and Apollo or PDL free tiers for firmographics, with no LinkedIn scraping), rank candidate hooks against an explicit rubric, refuse to personalise when no hook clears the bar, and send a short premise-value-CTA draft to a review queue where every factual phrase links back to its source. This matters for three reasons. Zamp sells "AI employees" to enterprises with heavy finance and operations workloads, and lists a GTM Associate among its roles, so the build doubles as a product demo ([zamp.ai](https://zamp.ai)). The closest public role, AI Solutions Analyst, is about turning requirements into workflows and reporting ROI, so the case study should show that full loop and not only text generation ([Kula: AI Solutions Analyst](https://careers.kula.ai/zamp-ai/19526)). And the documented weakness of today's AI SDRs is untrustworthy personalisation rather than missing personalisation. Vendor data consistently shows personalised cold email roughly doubling replies (from about 3% to about 7%), but shallow or unjustified personalisation does worse than none ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics); [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sdr-limitations-honest-assessment/)). A one-week build can win on traceability, hook selection, abstention and reviewer speed, which incumbents either leave undocumented or treat as optional.

## Zamp sells AI employees to finance-heavy enterprises, and its own catalogue includes a GTM Associate

## A free, legal signal stack exists, provided LinkedIn stays an input and never becomes a scrape target

Cost and legality both changed in 2025-2026. **Proxycurl shut down on 4 July 2025** after LinkedIn sued it, and its CEO wrote that "there is no winning in fighting this" ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)). LinkedIn's case against ProAPIs ended in a **consent judgment on 21 September 2026** that permanently bars scraping and orders the data archive deleted ([gblock.app](https://www.gblock.app/articles/linkedin-proapis-scraping-injunction-2026); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/linkedins-war-against-bot-scrapers-ramps-up-as-ai-gets-smarter)). The hiQ case is often cited as making public scraping safe, but hiQ ultimately paid $500,000 and accepted an injunction on contract grounds ([Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/is-linkedin-scraping-legal/)). LinkedIn also briefly banned the AI SDR company Artisan over data its vendors had scraped ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/yes-linkedin-banned-ai-agent-startup-artisan-but-now-its-back)). The practical rule for a solo builder: accept the prospect's LinkedIn URL as an input, get role and tenure from a licensed enrichment API, and find posts or talks through web search. Never fetch linkedin.com, and never run logged-in actors on Apify or Bright Data.

Several free options have also gone. Brave **removed its free search tier in February 2026**, and Microsoft shut down the Bing Search API in August 2025 ([Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/brave-drops-free-search-api-tier-puts-all-developers-on-metered-billing/)). Clearbit's free tools ended in April 2025 and its Logo API in December 2025 ([Clearbit](https://help.clearbit.com/hc/en-us/articles/31990018203031-Clearbit-Free-Tools-Sunset-April-30th-2025); [Abmatic](https://abmatic.ai/blog/clearbit-enrichment-api-2026)). Crunchbase's API now starts around **$500/month** ([NextGen Nexus](https://thenextgennexus.com/2026/05/14/crunchbase-killed-its-free-api-heres-how-to-rebuild-it-2026/)). The free tiers of NewsAPI.org and GNews are delayed by 12-24 hours and restricted to development or localhost use, so a public demo running on them would likely breach their terms and could miss "raised yesterday" news ([SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/best-google-news-apis-2026.md)). What remains is enough for a demo of about 50 prospects at about five queries each:

| Signal | Primary source | Free allowance | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| News, funding, exec statements | Tavily search/extract | 1,000 credits/month, no card ([Parallel.ai](https://parallel.ai/articles/best-free-web-search-api.md)) | Serper Google SERP/news JSON; Exa ($10/month free credits); Google News RSS for headlines |
| Company pages (about, blog, press) | Jina Reader `r.jina.ai/<url>` | Keyless, lower rate limits ([Rhumb](https://rhumb.dev/service/jina-ai)) | Firecrawl, 1,000 credits/month, 2 concurrent ([Parallel.ai](https://parallel.ai/articles/best-free-web-search-api.md)) |
| Hiring (e.g. AP/finance-ops roles) | Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby public job-board APIs | No auth; "no published rate limit" ([Greenhouse docs](https://developers.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [Jobspipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/free-job-api)) | JSearch 200 req/month; Adzuna 2,500/month ([Jobspipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/free-job-api)) |
| Person/company firmographics | Apollo API | Free plan 50/min, 600/day, credit-bound ([Apollo docs](https://docs.apollo.io/reference/rate-limits.md)) | People Data Labs, 100 lookups/month ([Pin.com](https://pin.com/blog/best-people-search-apis)) |
| Last-resort research step | Gemini grounding or Claude web search | Gemini 3.x: 5,000 grounded prompts/month free ([API Serpent](https://apiserpent.com/blog/reduce-llm-web-search-grounding-cost)); Claude: $10/1k searches ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/7/anthropic-api-search)) | Perplexity Sonar (no reliable free tier) ([Puter](https://developer.puter.com/tutorials/perplexity-api-pricing/)) |

Some details are uncertain and worth testing on day one. Sources disagree on whether Serper's 2,500 free queries are a one-time grant ([apicostcalc](https://apicostcalc.com/blog/exa-vs-tavily-vs-serper-vs-brave-web-search-api-cost.html)). Apollo's free monthly credit count, and whether people search works on a free key, are unconfirmed. Lever and Ashby endpoints are documented only through a third-party actor ([Apify](https://apify.com/oddsmith/ats-job-board.md)). Two gaps affect an India-based builder and remain open: foreign SaaS billing on Indian cards, and whether India's DPDP Act 2023 applies to processing prospects' personal data. Funding has no good free API, so pull it from news search and have the LLM extract round, amount and date, filtered by the company's domain and with the source URL shown. That guards against confusing similarly named companies, which is exactly the zamp.ai and zamp.com problem. An explicit retrieval layer is better than a single opaque grounded-search call here because it produces inspectable, cacheable evidence. For a live demo, pre-run and cache the golden prospects, put an approximately 8-second timeout on every source, skip a failing signal rather than fail the draft, and back off on 429 responses from Jina and Apollo.

## Evidence favours relevant premises, short bodies and interest CTAs, and punishes unjustified personalisation

Most of the quantitative evidence comes from vendors analysing their own customers' sends, so it is correlational. It is still consistent. Baseline B2B cold reply rates sit around **3.4-5%** ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)). Personalised emails in Belkins' 5.5M-send dataset replied at **7% vs 3%** ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)). What counts as personalisation depends on seniority. In Gong's analysis of 30,000+ prospecting emails, **company-level personalisation tripled replies for directors and above**, while individual-level personalisation more than doubled replies for non-managers but gave only a 50% lift for executives. Industry social proof added 88%, and **87% of buyers said sales emails don't address their challenges** ([Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/4-data-backed-ways-to-increase-your-email-reply-rate-and-book-that-meeting/)). Zamp's buyers are CFOs and controllers, so the best hooks will be account-level triggers that imply finance-ops pain, such as a posting for AP specialists, an ERP migration, a new controller or expansion into new entities, rather than a personal detail. Lavender's 231,818-email benchmark points the same way: finance buyers show a **79% reply lift for A-grade emails, but only 6.1% of finance emails earn an A** ([Lavender](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report)). The audience is hard to reach but rewards quality.

Nobody has published a controlled comparison of reply rates by signal type (funding vs new role vs job posting vs authored content). The trigger-specific figures, such as a 31% reply rate on a Series B campaign and 20% on a job-change campaign, are single case studies ([RevBoss](https://revboss.com/blog/2026-trigger-based-outreach-playbook)). The best available ordering is a practitioner framework from Becc Holland. It ranks self-authored content first, then engaged content, then self-described traits, then company information, and puts "junk drawer" facts such as hobbies last, as risky unless they connect to the value proposition ([PersistIQ](https://www.persistiq.com/?p=1778)). Freshness rules are similarly heuristic. RevBoss suggests high-intent signals decay within 48-72 hours, new-executive signals are best 10-21 days after the start date, and behavioural signals lose about half their weight by around 30 days ([RevBoss](https://revboss.com/blog/2026-trigger-based-outreach-playbook)). A sensible rubric gives full weight to signals under two weeks old and treats anything over 90 days as background rather than a "why now".

Format matters less than relevance, and the format evidence partly conflicts. Hunter's 34M emails show reply rates confined to a **3.4-4.5% band across all lengths, peaking at 20-39 words**, with top campaigns reaching 13-18% at lengths as different as 9 and 115 words ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-word-count)). Gong's blog claims 150+ words performs better but publishes no methodology for that claim ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-statistics)). Better-supported rules are more specific. In Gong's 304,174-email set, an **interest CTA** ("worth exploring?") beat a meeting ask at the cold stage, and specific time CTAs booked meetings only 15% of the time. **ROI language like "2x" cut success by 15%** ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-statistics)). Two-to-four-word subject lines had the best opens in Belkins' data at 46% ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)). **58% of replies arrive on step one**, so most of the effort belongs in the first touch ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/)). Holland's three-line structure (premise, then a value proposition tied to day-to-day pain, then a short CTA) fits a body of roughly 50-100 words ([PersistIQ](https://www.persistiq.com/?p=1778)).

The creepiness research is old but clear. In a 2008 *Marketing Letters* study, personalisation without a justification or a valuable offer produced a **"boomerang effect"**, while justified personalisation tied to value did not hurt however detailed it was ([University of Illinois](https://news.illinois.edu/personal-information-in-e-mail-marketing-can-backfire-study-indicates/)). Light personalisation is not inherently off-putting: Stanford and Chicago Booth field experiments found that putting the recipient's name in the subject line *reduced* unsubscribes by 17% ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/open-name-why-personalized-email-subject-lines-work)). Both studies used B2C samples, and I found no B2B study of sensitive-topic hooks. Excluding layoffs, health, family, politics and personal-life posts is therefore an editorial judgement extended from the boomerang finding, not a measured threshold. On AI tells, Instantly's own guidance says shallow openers "perform worse than no personalization" ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sdr-limitations-honest-assessment/)). An analyst brief reports collapsing replies to "I noticed you recently…" openers and three-bullet value propositions, though it publishes no data ([Starr Conspiracy](https://www.thestarrconspiracy.com/insights/trends/brief-ai-lead-generation-outbound)). The same brief also flags soft CTAs as an AI tell, which conflicts with Gong's finding that interest CTAs work best. The way through is to keep the interest CTA but word it specifically. If the prototype ever sends, it has to respect Gmail's bulk-sender rules (authentication, one-click unsubscribe, a spam-rate ceiling) ([Google](https://blog.google/products/gmail/gmail-security-authentication-spam-protection/)). A drafts-only demo can leave deliverability out of scope and say so explicitly.

## Incumbents pass research blobs to writers, and nobody documents ranking, abstaining or claim-level citations

Existing tools follow three UI archetypes. Table builders make each research step an inspectable cell. Clay's Claygents run per row, with waterfall enrichment across providers, and Clay markets a "glass box, not black box" view of agent decisions ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/claygent?rd=1); [Findymail](https://www.findymail.com/blog/clay-data-enrichment/)). Apollo's AI Research writes to typed fields (text, multi-line or a yes/no picklist), inserts them into sequences as variables, allows **three free previews per prompt** before a full credit-consuming run, and has a **"Show citations" checkbox** that appends sources ([Apollo KB](https://knowledge.apollo.io/hc/en-us/articles/29436100461837)). Unify runs agents inside "Plays" that feed "smart snippets", and also offers always-on monitoring ([Unify docs](https://docs.unifygtm.com/reference/agents/overview.md)). Chat-first autonomous agents are the second archetype. 11x's Alice 2.0 uses a supervisor with research, **positioning-report** and writer sub-agents built on LangGraph ([ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/rebuilding-an-ai-sdr-agent-with-multi-agent-architecture-for-enterprise-sales-automation)), and Regie "finds the why-now on every account" through a single chat interface ([G2](https://ai.g2.com/evaluations/categories/ai-sdr/regie-ai-regieone)). CRM-embedded agents are the third. Salesforce Agentforce SDR grounds on CRM fields and uploaded "Data Libraries" with a "Use only the available data" guardrail, shows scheduled emails on the lead timeline for "View, Edit, or Reschedule", and caps activation at 30 records at a time ([Salesforce guide](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/guides/Agentforce-for-Sales-Development-Implementation-Guide.pdf)). HubSpot Breeze offers per-message review or auto-send, and its setup guides recommend starting manual ([Octave](https://www.octavehq.com/post/hubspot-breeze-prospecting-agent-setup-guide)). Lavender takes a different approach: it scores and coaches a draft against data from billions of emails instead of only generating text ([Lavender](https://www.lavender.ai)). Common Room pushes signal summaries into Slack with one-click actions ([Common Room](https://www.commonroom.io/playbooks/auto-deliver-prospect-intel-to-reps-with-an-ai-agent/)).

The criticisms all point the same way: the personalisation can't be trusted. TechCrunch reported that 11x lost **70-80% of early customers**. ZoomInfo said the product "performed significantly worse than our SDR employees", and staff said customers had to verify its output manually because of hallucinations ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have)). One practitioner summed up the output as "Mad Libs with company names filled in" ([Tian Pan](https://tianpan.co/blog/2025-04-19-the-promise-and-pain-of-ai-sales-development-representatives-a-field-report)). Instantly lists invented product capabilities and fabricated certifications among documented hallucination types ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sdr-limitations-honest-assessment/)). Breeze emails "can feel generic" without data from outside HubSpot ([Octave](https://www.octavehq.com/post/hubspot-breeze-prospecting-agent-setup-guide)). Clay forum threads are titled "despite the high confidence claygent still shows…" ([Clay community](https://community.clay.com/x/support/cczx0bczxqul/despite-the-high-confidence-claygent-still-shows-r)). Specific hallucination-rate figures circulating in 2026 (12-18%, ">20%" for Alice) come from low-provenance aggregators and should not appear in a case study. The market has moved from "replace SDRs" toward "augment reps": Amplemarket frames its Duo assistant as built to "augment the rep" ([Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=48249)), and Instantly routes pricing, security, competitor and executive replies to humans ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sdr-limitations-honest-assessment/)). A review-centred prototype is going with the market, not against it.

In the documentation reviewed, three things are missing. Citations are an opt-in list attached to the research, never linked to individual sentences of the email ([Apollo KB](https://knowledge.apollo.io/hc/en-us/articles/29436100461837)). Hook selection happens implicitly inside the writer prompt, and 11x's positioning report is the nearest thing to an explicit step. Abstention happens only at reply time (Agentforce suggests a meeting with the owner) and never as a quality gate on the first touch ([Salesforce guide](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/guides/Agentforce-for-Sales-Development-Implementation-Guide.pdf)). Artisan, Smartlead and Regie's review UIs were not fully examined, so the accurate claim is "not documented", not "doesn't exist". That leaves a well-defined place to differentiate. **First, claim-level traceability:** every factual phrase in the draft links to a URL, snippet and fetched-at date, and any claim without a source is highlighted. **Second, explicit hook ranking:** generate three to five candidate hooks and score each on relevance to Zamp's offer (the heaviest weight), specificity, recency, fit to seniority, verifiability and safety. Show why the winner won and why the others lost. **Third, abstention:** when nothing clears the threshold, recommend a plain value-led email or deprioritising the prospect. Generic openers underperform no personalisation, so this beats faking a hook. **Fourth, a sensitivity filter:** block hooks about layoffs, health, family, politics, personal posts, rumours and stale news. **Fifth, a reviewer-speed queue:** sort by confidence, offer diff and edit, collect approve/reject reason codes that feed back into hook weights, and start in manual mode in the Breeze pattern. Apollo's preview-three-rows pattern and Lavender's score overlay are worth borrowing as they are.

## Conclusion

The main finding is that this case study is a trust-engineering problem, not a copywriting one. Signals are cheap and largely commoditised: funding news is mined by every vendor, and the free stack above reaches everything except LinkedIn. The scarce thing is a draft a finance leader's account owner can approve in seconds because they can see that the hook is real, recent, relevant and safe. That also matches what Zamp sells (agents "accountable for the outcome" at 99%+ claimed accuracy) and what its Solutions role is measured on (spec, test, rollout, ROI). The strongest submission therefore frames the build as a GTM Associate workflow spec and adds an evaluation layer. That layer should report a precision check on cited claims for a held-out prospect set, the share of prospects where the system correctly abstained, median reviewer time per draft, and edit distance between the draft and the approved version. Reply rate is out of reach in a week anyway.

One caution should shape the pitch. Almost every reply-rate figure in this space is a vendor correlation, and the signal-ranking and freshness rules are practitioner heuristics. The candidate should present the rubric weights as tunable hypotheses that reviewer reason codes will recalibrate, not as settled science. That framing is more honest, and it shows the product instinct of building the feedback loop into the system rather than claiming the system is already right.
